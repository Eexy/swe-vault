# Tautology and Contradiction

## Tautology

A **tautology** is a [[statement form]] that is always true regardless of the
truth values of the individual [[statement]] subsituted for its statement variable

A statement whose form is a tautology is a **tautological statement**

**example** : $p \lor \neg p$ is a tautology — "It is raining or it is not raining" is
always true

| p | ¬p | p ∨ ¬p |
|---|----|----|
| T | F | T |
| F | T | T |

## Contradiction

A **contradiction** is a [[statement form]] that is always false regardless of the
truth values of the individual [[statement]] subsituted for its statement variable

A statement whose form is a contradiction is a **contradictory statement**

**example** : $p \land \neg p$ is a contradiction — "It is raining and it is not
raining" is always false

| p | ¬p | p ∧ ¬p |
|---|----|----|
| T | F | F |
| F | T | F |
