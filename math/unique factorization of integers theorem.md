# Unique factorization of integers theorem

The unique factorization of [[integer]] theorem says that any integer greather
than 1 either is prime or can be written as a product of prime numbers in a way
that is unique except, perhaps, for the order in which the [[prime number|primes]] are written

Here is the theorem form :

Given any integer $n > 1$, there exist a positive integer $k$ distinct prime
numbers $p_1$, $p_2$,...,$p_3$ and positive integers $e_1$, $e_2$,..,$e_k$


$$
n = p_1^{e_1} p_2^{e_2} p_3^{e_3} \cdots p_k^{e_k}
$$

**example** : $360 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5^1$, so $p_1 = 2$, $p_2 = 3$, $p_3 = 5$ and $e_1 = 3$, $e_2 = 2$, $e_3 = 1$

No matter how you factor 360, you will always end up with exactly these primes raised to these exponents
