# Parity property

The **parity property** says that any two consecutive [[integer|integers]] have
opposite parity — one is [[even number|even]] and the other is [[odd number|odd]]

**example** : 4 and 5 are consecutive integers; 4 is even and 5 is odd

**Proof.** Suppose $m$ and $m + 1$ are particular but arbitrarily chosen
consecutive integers. We must show that one of $m$ and $m+1$ is even and that
the other is odd. Either $m$ is even or $m$ is odd — we break the proof into
two cases.

**Case 1 ($m$ is even):** In this case $m = 2k$ for some integer $k$, and so

$$
m + 1 = 2k + 1
$$

which is odd by definition of odd. Hence in this case, one of $m$ and $m+1$ is
even and the other is odd.

**Case 2 ($m$ is odd):** In this case $m = 2k + 1$ for some integer $k$, and so

$$
m + 1 = (2k + 1) + 1 = 2k + 2 = 2(k + 1)
$$

Since $k + 1$ is an integer, $m + 1$ equals twice some integer and is therefore
even. Hence in this case also, one of $m$ and $m+1$ is even and the other is odd.

It follows that regardless of which case occurs, one of $m$ and $m+1$ is even
and the other is odd. $\blacksquare$
